Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique that enhances LLM responses by retrieving relevant documents from a knowledge base before generating an answer. 
The pipeline has two phases: indexing and retrieval. During indexing, documents are split into chunks, converted to vector embeddings, and stored in a vector database. 
During retrieval, the user query is embedded and compared against stored vectors using cosine similarity to find the most relevant chunks. 
Chunking strategy significantly impacts RAG quality. Fixed-size chunking splits documents by a set number of tokens regardless of content boundaries. 
Semantic chunking splits at natural sentence or paragraph boundaries, preserving context. 
Poor chunking leads to incomplete context being retrieved, which causes hallucinations. 
Optimal chunk size depends on the embedding model and the nature of the documents.
